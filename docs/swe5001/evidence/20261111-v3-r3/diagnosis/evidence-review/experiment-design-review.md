**Finding: the unchanged dynamic V3 ramp is not a guaranteed linearity experiment. R3 cannot establish the criterion because it has no stable four-task measurement. The criterion is under-specified, not proven universally impossible.** This is an additive design review only: no traffic, cloud/code/issue changes, workload change, or earlier receipt amendment. GitHub issue reads succeeded on 11 November 2026.

## The arithmetic, with the denominator made explicit

The conventional interpretation of “four tasks at least 80% of ideal scaling from one” is `E = T4 / (4 × T1) ≥ 0.8`, equivalently `T4 ≥ 3.2 × T1`. `T1` and `T4` must be **comparable measured achieved throughput**, not offered RPS, policy targets, unrelated topology results, or arbitrary convenient ramp points. The sources do not supply a more exact formula; owner ratification is required before closure.

| Conditional one-task reference T1 | Minimum achieved four-task T4 | Efficiency if T4 actually equals 600 RPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 150 RPS | 480 RPS | 100% |
| 187.5 RPS | 600 RPS | 80% |
| 200 RPS | 640 RPS | 75% |
| 250 RPS | 800 RPS | 60% |

For a sustained stable window with at most 600 offered requests/s and no draining backlog, four-task achieved throughput cannot sustainably exceed 600. Thus a necessary condition under that ceiling is `T1 ≤ T4/3.2 ≤ 187.5 RPS`. Errors and other shortfalls reduce actual achieved goodput and therefore reduce this admissible reference further. Brief completion bursts from in-flight work can exceed instantaneous offered rate; they are not sustained capacity or a loophole in this bound.

The calculation `4 × 200 × 0.8 = 640` is correct **if** 200 achieved RPS is the approved, comparable one-task reference. It exposes a design mismatch under that assumption; it does **not** prove the published criterion is universally impossible, because `KNEE_RPS=200` currently means an old monolith-derived lower-bound workload candidate, not the prescribed measured distributed `T1`. Conversely, if a comparable current one-task sustainable capacity were independently shown to be **at least** 200, the unchanged 600-RPS experiment could not demonstrate 80% capacity scaling: 640 would be only a minimum requirement, potentially higher if capacity exceeds 200.

Do not choose an artificially low `T1` such as an early ramp point merely to fit under 600. If one task is demand-limited rather than capacity-limited, a ratio can establish only a pre-agreed finite workload/service-point comparison, not near-linear **capacity** scaling. A lower-bound-only single-task measurement does not bound its unknown maximum closely enough to prove a capacity-efficiency ratio.

## Why R3 and the current design do not establish it

- Verified R3 successful activities are **1→2→3→6**; service observations contain no stable four-running-task interval. Interpolating between three and six, dividing six-task throughput by 1.5, or treating a startup transient as a four-task plateau assumes the linearity being tested. None measures `T4`.
- One task at the start is necessary but insufficient: its offered load changes through the ramp, with warming, scaling-alarm delay and later task admission. A valid denominator requires predeclared comparable service/quality conditions and a representative stable interval, not a selectively convenient minute.
- The old policy target 12,000/target/minute corresponds to 200 target RPS: at 600 offered RPS, simple steady-state target arithmetic suggests three tasks, not a guaranteed four. R3's independently bound 6,000 target corresponds to 100 target RPS: the same arithmetic suggests six. These are policy-demand estimates, not capacity measurements or predictions of every intermediate ECS count; alarms, CPU guard, startup/slow start and replacement can skip or shorten any four-task window.
- Even a future valid dynamic run that visits four could remain demand-limited or fail to provide comparable settled windows. Its existing 726,000-arrival budget, zero-drop/all-run-error/event-relative-p95 gates and real automatic-growth requirement must stay unchanged. Current R3 independently fails errors/drops/collector completeness; a separate experiment cannot repair those outcomes.

## Owner decisions needed before another linearity claim

1. **Define the claim and denominator:** capacity scaling (`T1` a defensible current single-commerce-task sustainable-capacity reference) or scaling of an explicitly chosen finite workload/reference point. Decide whether the original plan intended a current distributed one-task baseline or a monolith-to-services comparison; the latter is a different, confounded claim unless explicitly accepted. Do not silently substitute the historical 200.
2. **Define throughput and quality:** ALB handled requests/s is not automatically successful valid browse operations/s. Approve a common counting rule, UTC window boundaries, successful-response/business-validation reconciliation, and latency/error requirements for both measurements. V3's existing client gates remain intact; the older V2 `<500 ms` calibration threshold versus V3 `<800 ms` must not silently become unequal conditions in a capacity ratio.
3. **Define stable windows:** duration, warmup/slow-start settling, ready/healthy/routable task count, image/revision/resource equality, exclusion of deployments/replacements, repeated trials, and uncertainty/tolerance. Three-minute latency exclusions do not by themselves certify settled capacity windows. Do not cherry-pick the best one/four minute after the fact.
4. **Approve scope and authorization:** accept “linearity not measured” alongside a separately reported dynamic-ramp result, or approve a separate controlled one/four-task experiment. If the chosen reference requires more than 600 RPS, authorize a distinct adequately driven experiment; do not raise this V3's cap, lower its knee, alter gates, or retune policies just to create the desired evidence.

## Proposed evidence package, without changing dynamic V3

- Keep the current V3 as the automatic scale-out/recovery experiment with its original workload and gates. Record its independent pass/fail/incomplete result and real activities. Do not force a four-task hold or manual growth into that measurement.
- If separately authorized, run a **distinct** controlled comparison at exactly one and exactly four healthy current commerce tasks, holding each count stable for the approved measurement. Any manual count setup/autoscaling suspension is declared experimental control, never automatic-scale-out evidence. It needs its own reviewed setup/preflight and restoration; current V3's one-task/active-1..6 preflight cannot simply validate a fixed-four baseline unchanged. No such tools or cloud actions are implemented/authorized by this review.
- Match browse mix, seed/detail-ID distribution, current image digest/task size/CPU/memory/pool settings, routing/cache policy, backing services/database and other workload. Match warmup policy; retain task/target identities and non-atomic-read limitations. The old monolith and changed task-size variants cannot silently serve as the per-task reference.
- For a capacity claim, determine or tightly bracket the one-task sustainable rate under the **same approved** quality criteria; preserve overload/failed exploratory stages rather than calling them accepted V3. Provide sufficient independently verified generator/network and database headroom to drive the four-task target. If only a finite reference is approved, label the result accordingly and do not claim a located knee or maximum capacity.
- Retain complete raw client counts/valid-success/error/latency/drop evidence and actual CLI exits, source/digest/route binding, exact stable-count/healthy-target timelines, original collector receipts, full aligned ALB `RequestCount Sum / period_seconds` for the correct commerce target group, errors and latency, CPU/memory/pool/database evidence, offered-versus-achieved rates, activity/deployment history and dashboard snapshots. Reconcile unrelated/setup/health/retry traffic, in-flight boundary effects and metric ingestion coverage; missing points are not zero. Show `T1`, `T4`, their windows, and `T4/(4T1)` with approved uncertainty treatment.
- Treat a passing independent linearity comparison and a passing dynamic V3 as separate evidence obligations. Neither repairs the other's failures. Until the owner agrees the denominator/protocol and measurements exist, report **“one-to-four ≥80% linearity not established; comparison design pending”**, not “criterion impossible” or “600 RPS proves scaling.”

**Actionable recommendation:** settle the denominator and claim scope before considering linearity closure or scheduling a comparison; preserve current V3 unchanged. R3 remains a failed dynamic run and cannot supply `T4` regardless of how the denominator ambiguity is resolved.
